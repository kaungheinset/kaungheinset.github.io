01 / CONTEXT

Project overview

Raw packet captures are full of signals that only become useful when placed in sequence. My network investigation exercises focused on identifying who communicated with whom, what services were involved, and why specific traffic mattered.

I worked with training PCAP datasets that included reconnaissance patterns, repeated authentication attempts, and suspicious application-layer exchanges.

02 / WORKFLOW

My approach

  • Baseline: identified prominent hosts, ports, protocols, and network conversations.
  • Filtering: used protocol and IP filters in Wireshark to isolate suspected activity.
  • Correlation: related TCP handshakes, DNS requests, HTTP exchanges, FTP sessions, and SMB traffic to an investigation timeline.
  • Reporting: separated observations from interpretations and documented the indicators supporting conclusions.

03 / OBSERVATIONS

Selected findings

01

Reconnaissance patterns

Identified scan behavior by examining connection sequences and completed versus unsuccessful TCP handshakes.

02

Authentication investigation

Analyzed repeated login attempts and abnormal connection patterns consistent with brute-force activity.

03

Application-layer evidence

Inspected FTP and HTTP activity, DNS lookups, and transferred artifacts to reconstruct events from training captures.

04 / NEXT ITERATION

What comes next

Publish sanitized PCAP screenshots with filter explanations, key packets, and a concise timeline for each scenario. Add detection ideas that translate the packet findings into actionable SOC hunting questions.
